VibeMail

A cozy Gmail client with its own sync engine

VibeMail started as a question: how much of a real product could I take from a blank page to a live URL? I took Gmail apart and rebuilt the parts I cared about. It signs you in with Google, copies your mail into its own database, stays current the moment anything changes, and shows it all in a front end that feels more like a village inn than an inbox.

  • Contract first. A written API contract (endpoints, error types, data model) came before the code, and the server, the tests and the interface all answer to it.
  • Careful with credentials. Google sign-in with encrypted tokens, strict per-user data isolation, and errors that say whether you can simply retry or need to sign in again.
  • Push, not poll. Gmail's change notifications keep your mailbox current without constant checking, and the sync repairs itself if it ever falls behind.
  • Proof, not promises. 450+ automated tests, including a suite that runs against a live database.
  • Polish you can feel. Keyboard and screen-reader friendly, a compose window that respects the phone keyboard, reduced-motion support, and a background-motion engine of my own that eases between speeds and directions. (This page borrows it. Try the Look controls up top.)

About

A short introduction.

I like finishing things. For me that means writing down what a piece of software should do before building it, proving it works with tests instead of hope, and shipping it somewhere real so other people can use it.

I'm drawn to the gap between "it works" and "it feels good": error messages that tell you what to do next, interfaces that work with a keyboard or a screen reader, and animation that knows when to stay still.
